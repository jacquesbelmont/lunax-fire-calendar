## Project name
LUNAX Fire Calendar

## Team
Team LUNAX: Jacques Belmont, Rafael Silva de Souza, Tiara Martell.

## Summary (50-200 words)
LUNAX Fire Calendar shows monthly satellite detections of active-fire hotspots in Rondônia, Brazil, from November 2000. NASA FIRMS provides observations from MODIS (1 km pixels) and VIIRS (375 m pixels). The sensors count differently, so joining their raw records creates an artificial break. We converted the early MODIS counts to the VIIRS scale using the years where both sensors overlap. The series uses observed VIIRS counts from February 2012 where available. In a backcast on 2012-2014, the median absolute percentage error across monthly statewide totals was 13.3% and the bias in the combined statewide total was +0.6%. Users select grid cells on a map, read a monthly calendar, compare a month with its historical median, and download the results. Grey marks missing values in the displayed series. The app labels estimates and provisional months. The archived data end on 2026-10-31.

## Detailed description

**The problem.** Emergency managers, scientists and land managers need to see when hotspot detections are typically high and how recent months compare with the past. NASA FIRMS has two long records of active-fire hotspots. MODIS on Terra (since November 2000) and Aqua (since July 2002) has 1 km pixels. VIIRS on Suomi NPP (since January 2012), NOAA-20 (since April 2018) and NOAA-21 (since January 2024) has 375 m pixels. Across the same area in 2012-2014, VIIRS counted about 3.8 times as many hotspots as MODIS. Splicing their raw counts would introduce an artificial break. Putting both records on the same scale preserves the historical comparison as VIIRS takes over from MODIS.

**Why use this app.** NASA FIRMS is built to show where fires are burning now: its global data are available within 3 hours of a satellite observation. It distributes MODIS and VIIRS as separate products, and the two count differently (VIIRS counted about 3.8 times as many hotspots in 2012-2014), so a person who wants to know whether a month is unusual has to reconcile them first. This app answers that planning question. It puts 312 months (2000-11 to 2026-10) on one scale across the change from MODIS to VIIRS, for grid cells, a drawn rectangle or one of the 52 municipalities, and compares each month with the same month in earlier years. Missing months are shown as missing and estimates are labeled. It does not replace the FIRMS Fire Map for today's fires: the app links to it.

**What we built.**
- **A harmonized monthly series on the VIIRS scale** for Rondônia, from 2000-11 to 2026-10: 376 active grid cells of 0.25 degrees and 312 months. Only detections inside the official state boundary (IBGE) are counted.
- **Both sensors are used.** From February 2012 the value is an observed VIIRS count (Suomi NPP, then NOAA-20 from April 2018). For the MODIS-only months (129 months with data) the value is the MODIS count (Terra + Aqua, or Terra alone) times a ratio for that month of the year. The ratios were learned on 2015-2025, where both sensors observe. They range from 1.45 to 4.63 for Terra + Aqua.
- **A web app.** Draw a rectangle on the map or enter coordinates. The app selects whole grid cells whose centres fall in the rectangle. It includes the detections in those cells that fall within Rondônia. The calendar shows one cell per month with a Viridis color ramp. The map colors the selected grid cells by the month shown in the comparison, with the same values and colors as the calendar, so users can see where in the area the hotspots of that month were; the individual detections of that month appear as points by sensor, grouped until the user zooms in. Clicking a month in the calendar shows it on the map. Users can choose a date range and a satellite series, then compare a month with the median for that month in earlier years. PNGs contain the charts. CSVs contain the monthly series, values per grid cell or individual hotspots and include the FIRMS acknowledgment and the data cut-off date.

- **One connected view.** The calendar, the fire season, the comparison, the map and the summary cards follow one focus month: the latest month, or the month the user clicks in the calendar or on a comparison bar. The calendar outlines the fire season of each year (dot = peak month), shows the median of each month on top and the total of each year on the right for years with all 12 months of data, and can color the cells by "Compared with the usual".
- **NASA satellite images on the map.** The map can show NASA Blue Marble or the NASA satellite image of one day (NASA GIBS), in true color or in false color, where burn scars appear deep or bright red (NASA Worldview layer description). The default day is the day of the map month with the most detections; the user can pick another day or click a detection to see the image of its day. The images stop at zoom level 9 (about 300 m per pixel); closer, they are only enlarged.

**For the people who fight fire (added 2026-11-15, DE-22 to DE-25).**
- **When:** the fire season of the chosen area for every year: the months in which 10% and 90% of the year's hotspots were reached and the peak month. For the whole state, the typical season (23 years) runs from August to October with the peak in September; it started in July in 4 years and ended in November in 1. The harmonized series makes the years before and after 2012 comparable.
- **Where:** one of the 52 municipalities of Rondônia (IBGE boundaries) can replace the rectangle; its series comes from the detections inside its boundary, and the 52 municipalities add up to the state total. A table ranks the municipalities for the month shown on the map.
- **How unusual:** a map view compares each grid cell with the median of the same month in its earlier years and marks it near usual, above or below usual, twice the usual or more, or half or less.
- **Where it burned before:** a map view of cells of about 1 km shows in how many of 13 years (2012-2025 without 2023) VIIRS detected fire. We fixed a test before looking: those places held 11.8% of the 2022, 2024 and 2025 detections, little more than the 10% any cells of the same number would hold, while the cells that burned most in the previous year held 28.0%. The app shows the layer as history and says that last year's fires were the better guide for the next season.
- **What to expect next season (experimental):** a forecast of next year's August-October hotspots for the selected area. Three methods were tested on 13 past seasons of the 52 municipalities, each forecast from earlier years only, with the rules fixed before looking. The previous year's August-October won (typical miss x1.5, against x2.2 for the median of earlier years); updating with January-July helped too little (9.8%, short of the 10% required). The app shows the outlook with the range of the area's own past errors, and the chart shows where it failed (2025).
- **Where to expect fire next season (experimental):** the chance of fire in each cell next year, from the cell's past, its neighbours and the 25-year harmonized record of its area. A statistical model and a machine-learning model were tested on eight past seasons with rules fixed in advance. At about 1 km the statistical model won: the 10% of cells it ranked highest held 57.1% of the next year's detections, against 52.8% for last year's busiest cells (63.7% against 45.4% in 2026). At about 5 km no model beat last year's fire, so that map shows last year's fire. We say which method won and where it is weak.
- **In the field:** KML download of the detections (Google Earth, Avenza, QGIS), "Print a briefing", and a link to the NASA FIRMS Fire Map on the selected area for today's fires.
- **Field tools:** a link that reopens the same view; the season so far against earlier years (Rondônia, by the end of September 2024: 73,848 hotspots, 6th highest of 18 years by that month); municipalities ranked by expected fire next year (Porto Velho holds 27.8% of Rondônia's expected cells with fire in 2027); fire events, where VIIRS detections at most 1 km and 1 day apart are chained into one event (the limits are not tuned); and the user's own area from a GeoJSON or KML file, which stays in the browser and is harmonized like the rest.

**A case near Porto Velho.** In a rectangle covering 33 cells, the app shows 309 observed hotspots in September 2025, compared with a median of 5,545 across 23 earlier Septembers. An environmental analyst can use that difference to identify a month worth checking in satellite imagery and field reports.

**How we checked the harmonization.** We fixed the selection protocol before seeing the results. We fitted the ratios on 2015-2025 and compared predictions from MODIS with observed VIIRS counts in 2012-2014. Using Terra and Aqua, the median absolute percentage error was 13.3% across 34 monthly statewide totals, the bias in the combined statewide total was +0.6%, and the monthly correlation was 0.998. The first proposed regression had a +143% bias, so the team selected the ratio. The ratio of NOAA-20 to Suomi NPP totals was 0.992 over 83 months. The ratio of NOAA-21 to NOAA-20 totals was 0.956 over 21 months. NOAA-21 is shown in its own view as a continuity check. The nominal 90% intervals covered 85% of monthly statewide totals in the test.

**Missing data and interpretation.** The pipeline flagged 33 of 919 satellite-months because at least one day was missing. The full harmonized series has 10 months without a value. The calendar uses grey for months without a value in the displayed series. It marks the estimates for available MODIS-only months. The Terra-only test had greater error than the Terra-and-Aqua test. A hotspot marks a thermal anomaly detected by a satellite. A single fire can be detected multiple times, and some fires may go undetected.

**Limits.** The data are archived FIRMS data that end on 2026-10-31, the last complete month when they were downloaded (2026-11-15), so the "latest month" in the app is October 2026, not the current month. August to October 2026 (VIIRS) and September and October 2026 (MODIS) are FIRMS near real-time data: FIRMS replaces them later with standard data, so their values can still change, and the app marks them provisional. The ratios are specific to Rondônia and must be refitted for any other area. A local pilot, not published, runs the same pipeline for the 27 Brazilian states with ratios fitted per state: 21 states pass the same backcast test, and in the other 6 (AC, RR, CE, PB, PE, DF) the MODIS years stay raw and are labeled not harmonized. The tool is not an official NASA product and must not be used for the preservation of life or property.

## AI use
Claude and Claude Code (Anthropic) assisted with the original work. Codex (OpenAI) reviewed the app and submission and helped revise app behavior, tests, documentation and the deck.
**Where and how:**
- writing and reviewing code (data pipeline, web app, tests);
- researching and comparing options for the method decisions, and running the analyses;
- drafting the documentation and the texts of this page.

The work is recorded with date, person, tool and files in AI_USE.md (https://github.com/jacquesbelmont/lunax-fire-calendar/blob/main/AI_USE.md). AI-assisted code carries a header comment. The `ai_use` field in `docs/data/metadata.json` describes the assistance used when producing the data.
**What AI did not do:** no images, video or audio were generated with AI, and no NASA logos, flags or mission identifiers are used.
**Where the ideas came from:** the team chose the challenge and set the goal from the challenge statement. The target scale (VIIRS-equivalent) was proposed by Rafael in the team chat and agreed by the team, and the demo area (Rondônia, our own region) was chosen by the team. For the method decisions, an AI assistant researched sources and drafted options with their pros and cons; the team chose among them, and the harmonization method was selected by a validation test fixed before the results were seen. Who chose each decision is recorded in method/DECISIONS.md. The fire-season, "Compared with the usual", municipality and "places that burn year after year" features were requested by Rafael after the core was built; an AI assistant proposed default thresholds, which Rafael and Jacques reviewed and adopted. The next-season outlook, the per-cell outlook and the connected analysis view were also requested by Rafael; an AI assistant proposed the method details and default values, and Jacques approved them in review before the deadline. The satellite-image layer (NASA GIBS) came from the team's task list; an AI assistant proposed the satellite dates and the default day. The field tools and the Brazil pilot were requested by Rafael (two of the tools follow ideas Jacques raised in the team chat, as reported by Rafael); an AI assistant proposed their thresholds, and Jacques approved them in review before the deadline.

## NASA data
NASA FIRMS (Fire Information for Resource Management System), the data resource of the challenge: https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms
- FIRMS country yearly summary CSVs (https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/country/) provide standard data for Brazil and Bolivia from 2000-11-01 to 2025-12-31. The FIRMS area API (https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/api/area/) provides NOAA-21 standard data from 2024-01-17 to 2025-12-31. For 2026-01-01 to 2026-10-31, the API provides standard and then near real-time data from four product families covering five satellites.
- Sensors: MODIS 1 km (Terra, Aqua) and VIIRS 375 m (Suomi NPP, NOAA-20; NOAA-21 as a check).
- Period: 2000-11-01 to 2026-10-31. Download dates: 2026-11-14 for the country files (re-run on 2026-11-15) and 2026-11-15 for the area API.
- Also used: the FIRMS list of days with missing data (https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/api/missing_data/?action=fetch_data).
- NASA GIBS (Global Imagery Browse Services) imagery on the map: Blue Marble and the daily corrected reflectance of Terra MODIS, Suomi NPP and NOAA-20 VIIRS, in true and false color, loaded in the browser from https://gibs.earthdata.nasa.gov (no key). We acknowledge the use of imagery provided by services from NASA's Global Imagery Browse Services (GIBS), part of NASA's Earth Science Data and Information System (ESDIS).
- Geographic coverage: Rondônia, Brazil.
- Details: data/PROVENANCE.md.
